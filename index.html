About me

Clarity from complexity. Vision into action.

My superpower is asking the right questions until alignment emerges. I dive deep into the problems businesses face, understand the needs of internal and external users, then bring clear vision and actionable requirements to the team for execution and iteration.

Property Management Platform
A B2B2C app with two connected experiences: one for landlords, one for tenants.
Building a B2B2C property management platform that serves both sides of the rental relationship. Landlords and property managers are the business customer and get a portfolio view with automated workflows and action-only alerts, while their tenants are the end users and get a simple, self-serve experience for everyday requests and communication. I'm leading product end to end, from requirements and PRD through prototype, working alongside Principal Engineer Jan Go, and I've drafted the pricing strategy for the platform.
Differentiation: Instead of stitching together separate tools, the platform aims to disrupt the Zillow-style model by managing everything from listing to lease creation, maintenance, and taxes in one unified, streamlined platform.
Target outcome: Save landlords roughly 10 to 20 hours during the first week of every month.

View pricing strategy

Built on a willingness-to-pay approach: decide who values what, choose the value metric, package into tiers, then set and test prices. All figures below are hypotheses to validate, not research-backed prices.

Segments: micro landlords (1-4 units), growing independents (5-19), and self-managing portfolios (20+).

Value metric to test first: per occupied unit per month, so a vacant unit costs less, compared against flat portfolio-band pricing.

Good / better / best tiers, named for the outcome:

  • Collect (get paid on time): renter portal, ACH, card and autopay, reminders, per-unit ledger. Hypothesis: $1-2 per occupied unit per month.
  • Protect (rent to the right tenants): adds screening workflow, utility billing, automated late-fee emails, and demand notice drafts with owner approval. Hypothesis: $3-5.
  • Tax-ready (close the year in an afternoon): adds vacancy tracking, maintenance cost log, tax-season report, and accountant package. Hypothesis: $5-8.

Principles: the tenant portal stays free for ACH so tenants never feel charged for the landlord's software, and payments revenue is a second lever. Next steps are landlord interviews, a Van Westendorp price survey, feature-ranking research, and a waitlist pricing-page test.
